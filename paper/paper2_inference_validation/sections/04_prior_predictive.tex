\section{Prior Predictive Checks}
\label{sec:prior_predictive}

A prior predictive check asks what the model predicts before it is conditioned
on any particular observation.  This is the first test of whether the inference
problem is well posed.  A formally valid posterior is not useful if the prior
places most of its weight on wind solutions that stall near launch, or if the
finite solutions occupy an observable range unrelated to real cool outflows.  In
this section we therefore sample the baseline three-loading prior, run the same
JAX forward model used by the inference calculations, and ask three questions:
what fraction of the prior produces valid observables, where do failures occur
in parameter space, and what range of column-density velocity profiles is
produced by the valid draws?

\subsection{Baseline prior volume}
\label{subsec:prior_predictive_setup}

The atlas reported here uses only the three baseline inference parameters,
\begin{equation}
\theta
=
\left(\eta_M,\ \eta_{M,\rm cold},\ \eta_E\right).
\end{equation}
For this exploratory check we draw the two mass loadings from log-uniform ranges
\begin{equation}
0.03 < \eta_M < 3,\qquad
10^{-3} < \eta_{M,\rm cold} < 10,
\end{equation}
and draw the hot energy loading from
\begin{equation}
0.05 < \eta_E < 0.999 .
\end{equation}
These bounds are intentionally broad.  They are not meant to be the final prior
for any individual galaxy, nor are they identical to the Gaussian prior used in
the later MAP and sampling calculations.  Their purpose is more basic: they
expose the physically useful region of the current model and reveal whether
broad but superficially reasonable loading combinations produce stalled or
non-finite winds.

Unless stated otherwise, the prior predictive runs use a fiducial galaxy with
$\dot M_\star=20\,M_\odot\,{\rm yr}^{-1}$, $r_\star=0.3\,{\rm kpc}$, and
$v_{\rm circ}=150\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$.  The forward model is integrated to
$30\,{\rm kpc}$ with 13 cloud-mass species spanning $1$--$10^6\,M_\odot$ and
the fiducial cloud mass-spectrum slope $\alpha_{\rm cl}=2$.  For each draw we
record the observable vector, the raw velocity moments, a validity flag, and the
first radius at which the trajectory becomes invalid.  Invalid samples remain
in the atlas tables, but their observables are not included in the valid
predictive distributions.  This setup is closer to the intended production
forward model than the reduced CPU grid used later for exact synthetic recovery.
The comparison is therefore useful but not one-to-one: the prior predictive
atlas asks what the full fiducial model can produce, while the exact recovery
tests use a shorter, cheaper grid for repeated sampler diagnostics.

The same atlas machinery already supports the restricted expanded coordinates
discussed later, including
$(\eta_M,\eta_{M,\rm cold},\eta_E,A_{\rm mix})$ and the staged
$\beta_{\chi,\rm mix}$ diagnostic.  Those runs are not included in this section.
They constitute the next validation gate after the baseline three-loading
model has been tested.

Figure~\ref{fig:prior_predictive_validity} shows the basic validity structure.
In the 200-sample shape-summary atlas, 84 samples produce finite observables,
for a valid fraction of 0.42.  In the independent 100-sample binned-profile
atlas used for the $dN/dv$ envelope below, 50 samples are valid.  These are
finite-sample estimates, not calibrated occurrence rates.  The two runs use
different random draws and observable vectors, so the exact fraction should not
be over-interpreted.  The robust result is the geometry of the valid region.
Invalid samples are not distributed randomly through the prior.  They are
concentrated toward larger cold loading and, more weakly, toward larger hot mass
loading.  In the shape-summary atlas, the median valid cold loading is
$\eta_{M,\rm cold}\simeq 0.013$, whereas the median invalid cold loading is
$\eta_{M,\rm cold}\simeq 0.78$.  The corresponding medians for the hot mass
loading are $\eta_M\simeq 0.11$ and $\eta_M\simeq 0.51$.

This is the expected physical behavior.  Large cold loading increases the cool
mass that must be accelerated and mixed, while large hot mass loading raises
the density of the hot wind and strengthens radiative losses.  High
$\eta_E/\eta_M$ can help the wind reach large velocity, but it does not by
itself guarantee a valid cool absorber because the same loading factors also
set the density, cooling time, drag, and cloud survival.  Some of these
solutions therefore cool, decelerate, or become non-finite soon after launch.
The median first invalid radius in the shape atlas is only
$0.32\,{\rm kpc}$, just outside the adopted launch radius.  Thus the validity
map is already a physical result: a broad phenomenological loading prior spends
substantial volume on systems that do not produce a useful large-radius absorber
in this model.

\subsection{Observable range of valid winds}
\label{subsec:prior_predictive_observables}

The valid subset spans a broad but interpretable range of cool-phase
observables.  In the shape-summary atlas the total column scale covers
$M_0\simeq 3.6\times10^{17}$ to
$2.4\times10^{20}\,{\rm cm}^{-2}$, with a median
$M_0\simeq 8.8\times10^{18}\,{\rm cm}^{-2}$.  The central 68 percent of valid
draws span $1.3\times10^{18}$--$4.0\times10^{19}\,{\rm cm}^{-2}$.  The mean
outflow velocity ranges from about $260$ to
$650\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$, with a median near $390\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$ and a
central 68 percent range of $330$--$510\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$.  The velocity width
ranges from about $60$ to $250\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$, with the central 68 percent
between roughly $130$ and $200\,{\rm km\,s^{-1}}$.  These ranges are not tuned
to any individual absorber.  They show that the baseline prior generates both
weak and strong cool columns, as well as both narrow and moderately broad
profiles, before any likelihood is applied.

Figure~\ref{fig:prior_predictive_observable_histograms} shows the same result
graphically.  The distributions are broad, but they are not featureless.  The
valid prior does not simply map into arbitrary combinations of column and
velocity.  High-column profiles require enough cold material to survive to the
observable radius, but not so much that the cold phase drains the hot flow.
High-velocity profiles require large specific energy, but the energy loading
alone does not determine the profile because $\eta_M$ sets both the launch speed
through $\eta_E/\eta_M$ and the density scale that controls cooling and
coupling.  This coupling is the first indication that the posterior will contain
curved degeneracies rather than independent constraints on the three loading
parameters.

\subsection{Profile-level envelope}
\label{subsec:prior_predictive_dndv}

The compact summaries are useful for quick validation, but absorption data are
ultimately profile measurements.  We therefore repeat the prior predictive
exercise with a binned $dN/dv$ observable and compare the resulting envelope to
processed CLASSY profiles in Figure~\ref{fig:prior_predictive_dndv_envelope}.
The model envelope is still evaluated for one fixed fiducial galaxy, whereas
the gray profiles span galaxies with different star formation rates, sizes,
stellar masses, and circular velocities.  This is not a fit and should not be
read as a rejection test.  It is a scale check: does the prior generate cool
absorbers with columns and velocities in the same broad regime as the data that
will eventually be modeled object by object?

\label{fig:prior_predictive_dndv_envelope}

The profile envelope reinforces two points.  First, the model naturally
populates the velocity range where cool outflow absorption is measured.  The
valid prior samples produce profiles with substantial column over several
hundred ${\rm km\,s^{-1}}$, and some draws generate extended high-velocity
wings.  Second, the low-signal edges of the profile are intrinsically delicate.
The model has finite support set by the cloud velocities and survival radii,
while a Gaussian binning kernel can leak small amounts of column outside that
support.  This detail is nearly invisible in moment summaries but becomes
important for log-profile likelihoods, where tiny columns in edge bins can
carry large apparent residuals.  This is why the support-aware kernels and
low-signal log-profile treatments are kept as diagnostics until exact recovery
tests show that they improve inference for physical reasons rather than by
hiding edge-bin tension.

\subsection{Decision for the baseline inference}
\label{subsec:prior_predictive_decision}

The baseline prior predictive check passes the limited test needed to proceed
to synthetic recovery.  The valid prior produces cool-column scales, velocities,
and widths in a physically relevant regime, and the invalid samples fail in
interpretable regions of loading space rather than through unexplained numerical
pathologies.  At the same time, the check also shows why the rest of the paper
must remain a validation study.  The broad prior is too permissive to be treated
as a final object-level prior, and the validity structure is sharp enough that
sampler behavior can be affected by failed or stalled winds if those cases are
handled as hard cliffs.

We therefore use the prior predictive atlas as a gate, not as a final tuning
step.  It justifies exact synthetic recovery for the three loading parameters,
but it does not justify adding cloud-wind microphysics parameters yet.  The
implementation can already generate a restricted $A_{\rm mix}$ atlas, but the
scientific claim has not been earned until that four-parameter prior predictive
check is run and interpreted.  In particular, the $A_{\rm mix}$ experiment
discussed later must show that adding an effective mixing amplitude does not
simply enlarge the invalid region or absorb cloud-population assumptions that
are already visible in the baseline prior predictive map.
